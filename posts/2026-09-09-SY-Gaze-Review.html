Daily Concept: Gaze patterns in human-AI interaction

1. What is it?
Gaze patterns in human-AI interaction refer to the spatial and temporal characteristics of eye movements when humans read, evaluate, or generate text with Large Language Models. These patterns capture where users look, for how long, and in what sequence while interacting with AI interfaces or assessing AI-generated outputs.

2. How does it work?
Eye-tracking hardware captures raw gaze coordinates at high frequencies and maps them to specific text tokens or UI elements on a screen. By calculating fixation durations \( f_d \) and saccade amplitudes \( s_a \), researchers quantify cognitive load and attention allocation. For instance, regression saccades, which are backward eye movements to previous text, often indicate comprehension difficulty or surprise at an AI response. These gaze signals can be integrated into multimodal models by treating human attention as a prior probability distribution over tokens \( P(token_i | gaze) \).

3. Why does it matter?
This matters because gaze data provides an implicit feedback signal that reveals user intent, trust, and cognitive state without requiring explicit ratings. Integrating this into LLM research allows for the development of adaptive interfaces that dynamically adjust model outputs based on real-time user confusion or engagement. It also helps evaluate the interpretability and alignment of AI systems by showing exactly which parts of a generated response trigger human scrutiny.

4. Key insight
Human gaze serves as a continuous, physiological evaluation metric for LLM outputs, bridging the gap between black-box model behavior and human cognitive processing.

Paper Summaries

Do Cantonese-Adapted Language Models Better Predict Cantonese Reading? A Cross-Model Eye-Tracking Evaluation

Authors: Ziqi Zhang, Emmanuele Chersoni, Mohammad Momenian
arXiv: 2609.02163v2

Summary

1. Research Question
Does language-variety-specific training in language models, specifically for Cantonese, improve their ability to predict human reading behavior compared to general-purpose or Mandarin-oriented models?

2. Positioning
Information-theoretic measures from autoregressive LLMs are commonly used to model human reading expectations. However, it remains unclear whether adapting models to specific language varieties improves this psycholinguistic alignment, especially given mixed NLP evaluation results for Cantonese. This work fills the gap by directly testing these models against naturalistic human eye-tracking data.

3. Key Contribution
The paper provides an empirical evaluation of how varying degrees of Cantonese-specific continued pretraining affect the alignment of LLMs with human reading patterns. It demonstrates that more extensive adaptation generally yields a better predictive fit for metrics like lexical surprisal. The authors also reveal that model rankings fluctuate depending on the specific information-theoretic measure applied.

4. Methodology
The researchers analyzed naturalistic Cantonese eye-tracking data using two pairs of models representing different adaptation levels. They extracted four information-theoretic metrics from each model including lexical surprisal, POS surprisal, pre-target entropy, and entropy reduction. These metrics were then evaluated to determine which model best predicted human gaze behavior.

5. Limitations
The study is limited to only two pairs of model families, which may not represent the full landscape of Cantonese or multilingual LLMs. Additionally, the predictive fit is highly metric-dependent, meaning a model's psycholinguistic alignment cannot be judged by a single information-theoretic measure. The eye-tracking data, while naturalistic, is restricted to Cantonese, leaving cross-linguistic generalizations untested.

6. Critical Evaluation
This work is a valuable addition to the intersection of psycholinguistics and NLP, particularly for low-resource languages like Cantonese. Its strength lies in the controlled within-family model comparisons, isolating the effect of language-specific training. However, a weakness is the conflicting metric outcomes, such as entropy reduction favoring a less adapted model, which complicates the conclusion that more training universally improves cognitive alignment.

Attention Capture Is Not Detection: A Two-Stage Account of How Humans Miss Localized AI Image Edits

Authors: Chiao-Chieh Deng
arXiv: 2608.13865v1

Summary

1. Research Question
This paper investigates whether human detection of localized AI image edits is a single undifferentiated process or a multi-stage phenomenon. Specifically, it asks whether attention capture and final judgment accuracy are governed by distinct visual and semantic factors.

2. Positioning
The work challenges existing content moderation platforms that treat AI edit detectability as a binary property. It bridges visual attention research with AI-driven disinformation by extending the classic distinction between pre-attentive capture and effortful recognition into the new domain of AI-generated image edits. This fills a gap in understanding exactly how and why humans fail to notice or correctly judge fake images.

3. Key Contribution
The main contribution is a two-stage account demonstrating that attention capture and judgment accuracy are dissociable. The authors show that edit area drives attention capture while semantic plausibility drives judgment accuracy and look-but-fail-to-see errors. Additionally, they provide a computational operationalization of the attention-capture stage using a Transformer model to generate human-like scanpaths.

4. Methodology
The researchers conducted a controlled eye-tracking study with \(N=59\) participants using a Latin-square design that crossed edit area and semantic plausibility. They analyzed the gaze data using mixed-effects models to isolate the factors driving attention and judgment. Finally, they trained a Transformer model to generate scanpaths and predict look-but-fail-to-see incidence, comparing its performance against a linear baseline.

5. Limitations
The authors transparently acknowledge that their machine learning evaluation used a single fixed train/validation split rather than a more rigorous leave-one-subject-out cross-validation. Furthermore, the Transformer model only modestly outperformed a simple two-parameter linear baseline on the harder task of predicting look-but-fail-to-see errors. The model also lacked access to semantic plausibility labels which inherently limited its predictive power.

6. Critical Evaluation
The study is highly commendable for its rigorous experimental design and responsible communication of machine learning limitations. Its primary strength lies in successfully bridging cognitive science and AI disinformation research to prove that looking is not the same as seeing. However, the computational modeling component is slightly underwhelming given the single split evaluation and the marginal improvement over a basic linear baseline.

Gaze Target Estimation Anywhere with Concepts

Authors: Xu Cao, Houze Yang, Vipin Gunda et al.
arXiv: 2608.11367v1

Summary

1. Research Question
How can we make gaze target estimation more flexible and robust by removing the need for brittle, multi-stage pipelines? The paper asks if gaze estimation can be conditioned on natural language or visual prompts, such as "the boy in the red shirt" or a point at \([0.52, 0.48]\), to identify a specific subject directly.

2. Positioning
Existing gaze estimation methods rely on rigid, multi-stage pipelines that require explicit inputs like head bounding boxes and human pose. These approaches are prone to cascading detection errors and lack the flexibility of natural language prompting seen in modern vision-language models. This work fills the gap by integrating subject localization and gaze estimation into a single, prompt-driven framework.

3. Key Contribution
The main contribution is the formulation of the Promptable Gaze Target Estimation (PGE) task, which allows users to specify gaze analysis targets via text or visual prompts. The authors also introduce the Gaze-Co dataset containing \(120,000\) high-quality, prompt-annotated image pairs. Furthermore, they propose GazeAnywhere, a novel transformer-based architecture that sets a strong baseline for this new task.

4. Methodology
The proposed GazeAnywhere model uses a transformer-based detector that fuses features from frozen image encoders. It simultaneously performs subject localization, determines if the gaze target is in or out of frame, and estimates a gaze target heatmap. This is trained on the newly generated Gaze-Co dataset, which uses a scalable data engine to create text and visual prompt annotations.

5. Limitations
The abstract notes that while the model performs well, out-of-domain real-world clinical datasets remain highly challenging. Potential limitations also include the reliance on the proposed scalable data engine for generating prompt annotations, which may introduce biases if the automated annotations are imperfect. Additionally, the use of frozen encoders might limit the model from fully adapting to highly specialized or novel gaze patterns.

6. Critical Evaluation
This work is a significant step forward for gaze estimation, successfully bridging the gap between traditional computer vision pipelines and modern, promptable interfaces used in LLMs. The strength lies in its unified, end-to-end design that eliminates brittle intermediate stages. However, its performance on highly specialized out-of-domain data, such as clinical settings, suggests that further fine-tuning or adaptive mechanisms might be necessary for universal applicability.

Hidden In Plain Gaze: Gaze Representations as Privacy Controls for Utility and Re-identification Risk in XR

Authors: Cory Ilo, Brendan-David John, Doug A. Bowman
arXiv: 2609.04592v1

Summary

1. Research Question
The paper investigates whether the choice of gaze data representation can serve as a lightweight privacy control in extended reality (XR) systems. Specifically, it asks how different gaze representations affect the tradeoff between task utility and biometric identity leakage.

2. Positioning
Existing privacy research often relies on adding perturbations or formal privacy mechanisms after data collection. This work positions itself earlier in the pipeline by examining whether simple data abstraction at the feature extraction stage can mitigate privacy risks. It fills a gap by treating gaze representation choice as a transparent and auditable privacy lever rather than relying solely on post-processing techniques.

3. Key Contribution
The main contribution is the empirical demonstration that engineered eye-movement features can retain \(85\%\) of the action recognition accuracy of raw gaze data while reducing user re-identification by an order of magnitude. The authors show that choosing an abstracted gaze representation provides an interpretable privacy control that complements formal mechanisms like differential privacy. This reveals that while abstraction attenuates identity leakage, it does not entirely eliminate it.

4. Methodology
The researchers used the egocentric HoloAssist dataset to compare three gaze representations under matched model capacity. They evaluated raw gaze, spatial attention heatmaps, and engineered eye-movement features. The study measured task utility through action recognition and privacy leakage through closed-set user re-identification across \(206\) identities.

5. Limitations
The authors acknowledge that using engineered features only attenuates rather than completely eliminates identity leakage. The evaluation is also limited to closed-set re-identification and a single dataset, which may not fully capture the complexities of open-world XR scenarios. Furthermore, while abstraction reduces risk, it still requires subsequent formal privacy mechanisms to ensure robust protection.

6. Critical Evaluation
A major strength of this work is its practical approach to privacy by design, offering developers a simple and transparent lever before needing complex perturbation algorithms. The findings are highly relevant for XR and multimodal LLM systems that use gaze for human-computer interaction. However, a weakness is that the residual re-identification rate remains at roughly \(4\) times chance, suggesting that representation choice alone is insufficient for high-stakes privacy applications.

LandmarkLens: Predicting and Presenting Effective Landmarks for Mixed-Reality Urban Exploration

Authors: Chu Li, Yotam Sechayk, Jared Hwang et al.
arXiv: 2608.30142v1

Summary

1. Research Question
How do gaze behaviors and landmark preferences differ between individuals with good and poor sense of direction. Can a mixed-reality system using a vision-language model improve spatial learning for those with poor navigational abilities?

2. Positioning
This work sits at the intersection of spatial cognition, eye-tracking, and AI-assisted navigation. It addresses a gap in current navigation tools that prioritize route efficiency over the development of cognitive maps. The research specifically targets users who struggle with spatial learning due to a poor sense of direction.

3. Key Contribution
The main contribution is LandmarkLens, a mixed-reality navigation system that leverages a vision-language model to identify and highlight effective landmarks. The authors empirically demonstrate that individuals with good and poor sense of direction exhibit systematic differences in eye-tracking gaze behavior and landmark preferences. The system uses these insights to successfully improve spatial knowledge acquisition for poor navigators through guided landmark attention.

4. Methodology
The researchers conducted a virtual reality study with \(20\) participants to collect eye-tracking gaze data and verbal landmark descriptions across four Tokyo neighborhoods. They used these empirical gaze and linguistic insights to inform a vision-language model within a mixed-reality interface that detects and highlights navigation-relevant landmarks. A follow-up study with \(8\) poor sense of direction participants evaluated the system through scene recognition tasks.

5. Limitations
The evaluation of the system is limited to a small sample size of \(8\) participants with a poor sense of direction. The study was conducted in virtual and mixed reality environments, which may not fully capture the complexities and distractions of real-world urban navigation. Furthermore, the computational cost and latency of the vision-language model are not detailed, which could be critical for real-time mixed-reality applications.

6. Critical Evaluation
The integration of eye-tracking insights with modern vision-language models is a highly innovative approach to accessibility in spatial navigation. A major strength is grounding the AI system design in empirical gaze behavior rather than relying on generic object detection. However, a weakness is the reliance on virtual reality for the foundational study and the limited sample size in the mixed-reality validation, leaving questions about real-world scalability and generalization to other cities.
