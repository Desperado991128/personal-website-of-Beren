Daily Concept: Fixation detection algorithms

1. What is it?
Fixation detection algorithms are computational methods used to identify periods when the human eye remains relatively still on a specific spatial location. In gaze research, these algorithms process raw eye-tracking coordinate streams to separate meaningful fixations from rapid eye movements called saccades. This segmentation transforms noisy spatial data into discrete cognitive events that indicate where a person directed their attention.

2. How does it work?
These algorithms typically evaluate the spatial dispersion or temporal velocity of sequential gaze points. For example, a velocity-based algorithm calculates the instantaneous speed of the eye between consecutive samples using a formula like \(v_i = \frac{\|p_i - p_{i-1}\|}{\Delta t}\). If the velocity \(v_i\) falls below a predefined threshold \(v_{thresh}\) for a minimum duration, those points are grouped into a single fixation. Conversely, dispersion-based methods cluster points within a sliding time window by measuring their maximum spatial spread. Both approaches ultimately output a sequence of centroid coordinates and dwell times.

3. Why does it matter?
In the context of LLMs, fixation detection bridges raw physiological data and semantic understanding by mapping where a reader focused their attention while processing a text prompt. Researchers use these extracted fixations to align human cognitive effort with specific tokens or words within a model. This alignment enables LLMs to be fine-tuned on human attention patterns, potentially improving model efficiency, interpretability, or alignment with human reading strategies.

4. Key insight
Fixation detection is the critical preprocessing step that converts continuous, noisy eye movements into discrete tokens of human attention. This transformation allows language models to consume gaze data directly alongside text.

Paper Summaries

EyeQ: Exploring Corneal Reflection for Mobile Context Sensing

Authors: Jaewook Lee, Judith Amores, Donghoon Shin et al.
arXiv: 2611.13640v1

Summary

1. Research Question
Can a standard smartphone front-facing camera capture corneal reflections with enough detail for Vision-Language Models to extract meaningful environmental context without relying on world-facing cameras?

2. Positioning
Traditional mobile context sensing relies on world-facing cameras that require explicit aiming or complex dual-camera hardware setups. This work shifts the paradigm in Human-Computer Interaction by treating the eye not merely as a target for gaze estimation but as a passive mirror of the surrounding environment.

3. Key Contribution
The primary contribution is EyeQ, a system that pairs on-device iris-guided capture with server-side AI enhancement to make corneal reflections readable by off-the-shelf Vision-Language Models. It demonstrates that VLMs can successfully extract scene types and objects from warped eye-image sequences, enabling applications like distinguishing drivers from passengers by detecting a steering wheel.

4. Methodology
EyeQ uses a mobile device to capture high-quality iris and corneal crops, which are then processed by a server-side enhancement model to recover fine details. The enhanced images are fed into an off-the-shelf VLM to identify scene context, objects, and text. The system was evaluated through an \(N=10\) participant study to assess reflection fidelity under various capture conditions and evaluate VLM detection accuracy.

5. Limitations
The study acknowledges that mobile capture conditions heavily influence reflection fidelity, which can lead to VLM misclassifications and visual hallucinations. Additionally, extracting environmental context from a user's eye raises significant privacy tensions, as the system passively captures sensitive visual data from the surroundings.

6. Critical Evaluation
EyeQ presents a highly innovative intersection of gaze tracking and Large Language Models by creatively repurposing corneal reflections for environmental sensing. Its reliance on server-side enhancement and VLMs means it currently faces practical limitations regarding latency and hallucination risks. However, it successfully opens a compelling new research direction for passive context-aware mobile interactions.

Leveraging Psychophysical Attentional Distribution for Gaze-Augmented Reward Modeling

Authors: Chaeho Lee, Junyup Kim, Wansoo Kim et al.
arXiv: 2611.13477v1

Summary

1. Research Question
How can we improve reward models for Large Language Models using human gaze data? The paper aims to align LLM outputs with human preferences by incorporating cognitive psychology principles into eye-tracking augmented reward modeling.

2. Positioning
This work builds on recent efforts that use eye-tracking data as a supervisory signal in Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). It fills a gap in existing fixation-centric models by moving beyond simple gaze points to incorporate established findings from cognitive psychology on human letter recognition.

3. Key Contribution
The main contribution is a novel representation of visual attention as a spatially graded distribution centered on the current gaze location. Integrating this psychophysical attentional distribution into reward model training yields higher preference prediction accuracy than baseline models. Furthermore, the model behavior aligns qualitatively with known cognitive patterns of human attention during reading.

4. Methodology
The authors model visual attention based on psychophysical principles of letter recognition, creating a spatially graded distribution around the tracked gaze coordinates. This gaze distribution representation is then integrated into the training process of a reward model. The trained model is evaluated against a baseline to measure improvements in preference prediction accuracy.

5. Limitations
The abstract does not explicitly list limitations. However, relying on eye-tracking hardware inherently restricts the scalability of data collection for this method. Additionally, modeling attention purely as a spatial distribution around a fixation point might oversimplify the complex temporal dynamics of human reading behavior.

6. Critical Evaluation
This work is a promising step toward grounding LLM alignment in human cognitive processes, bridging the gap between psychophysics and machine learning. Its strength lies in the interpretable nature of the attention distribution, which validates the model against established reading behaviors. A weakness is the potential barrier to entry, as specialized eye-tracking equipment limits widespread adoption compared to standard click-based feedback.

EgoHMP: Achieving Precise Human Motion Prediction in 3D Scenes via Egocentric Cues

Authors: Xin Zhao, Pengzhan Zhou, Ziyi Li et al.
arXiv: 2611.13654v1

Summary

1. Research Question
How can we accurately predict human motion in 3D scenes without relying on noisy gaze signals? The paper aims to solve the challenge of modeling complex interactive dependencies between human behavior and 3D environments.

2. Positioning
Existing human motion prediction methods often use eye tracking to infer intent. However, gaze data suffers from ambiguity, drift, and requires specialized hardware. This work shifts the focus from explicit gaze tracking to egocentric views and head motions, positioning these as more robust cognitive precursors to interaction intent.

3. Key Contribution
The main contribution is the EgoHMP framework, which replaces fragile gaze signals with egocentric visual cues and head motion to predict future interactions. It introduces a novel pipeline that aligns vision and motion, predicts an interaction probability map, and uses a contact aware diffusion decoder with trajectory self prompting to generate physically plausible motions.

4. Methodology
EgoHMP uses a vision motion alignment encoder to map visual and motion features into a shared space. An egocentric intent predictor then generates an interaction probability map \(P_{interaction}\) by suppressing invalid cues. Finally, a contact aware motion decoder uses a predicted global trajectory as a self prompt for a diffusion model, similar to prompt conditioning in large language models, followed by a multi stage denoiser to synthesize stable motions.

5. Limitations
The abstract does not explicitly state limitations, but relying on egocentric views still necessitates a wearable camera setup. Additionally, while it bypasses eye tracking hardware, predicting accurate global trajectories in highly dynamic or unseen environments might remain challenging. The computational cost of the multi stage diffusion process is also a potential bottleneck.

6. Critical Evaluation
EgoHMP is a strong contribution because it cleverly bypasses the hardware constraints and noise associated with traditional eye tracking by leveraging head motion and egocentric vision. The use of trajectory self prompting to guide the diffusion model mirrors conditioning techniques used in large language models and offers an elegant solution to optimization conflicts. However, the framework may still struggle with the inherent latency of diffusion models and the requirement of synchronized egocentric video inputs.
