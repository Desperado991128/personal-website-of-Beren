Daily Concept: Gaze-based intention recognition

1. What is it?
Gaze-based intention recognition is the process of inferring a user's underlying goals or next actions by analyzing their eye movement patterns. In the context of large language models, it involves using eye-tracking data as a supplementary input modality to understand what a user wants to do or say before they explicitly type or speak it. This approach bridges the gap between passive visual attention and active prompt generation.

2. How does it work?
The system extracts temporal and spatial features from raw eye-tracking data, such as fixations, saccades, and pupil dilation. These features are encoded into a latent representation using a recurrent neural network or Transformer architecture. The encoded gaze vector \( z_t \) is then fused with text embeddings \( e_t \) to condition the language model output. The model computes the probability of the next token \( w_{t+1} \) given the text context and gaze features using \( P(w_{t+1} | w_{1:t}, z_t) \propto \exp(h^\top E_{w_{t+1}}) \), where \( h \) is the hidden state influenced by both modalities.

3. Why does it matter?
Integrating gaze signals into LLMs allows for more proactive, accessible, and context-aware human-computer interaction. It enables systems to predict user needs based on visual attention, reducing the cognitive load required to formulate explicit prompts. Furthermore, gaze data provides a rich, continuous stream of implicit feedback that can help align language model outputs with human intent in real time.

4. Key insight
Eye movements serve as a real time proxy for cognitive state, meaning gaze data can act as a continuous, implicit prompt that guides LLMs toward human intent before a single word is typed.

Paper Summaries

Do Cantonese-Adapted Language Models Better Predict Cantonese Reading? A Cross-Model Eye-Tracking Evaluation

Authors: Ziqi Zhang, Emmanuele Chersoni, Mohammad Momenian
arXiv: 2609.02163v1

Summary

1. Research Question
Does adapting language models specifically for Cantonese improve their ability to predict human reading patterns compared to general-purpose or Mandarin-oriented models? The core question asks whether language-variety-specific training enhances psycholinguistic alignment with human gaze data.

2. Positioning
Information-theoretic measures from autoregressive language models are widely used to model human reading expectations. However, it remains unclear if training models on specific language varieties like Cantonese improves this alignment, especially given mixed NLP results. This work fills the gap by directly evaluating Cantonese-adapted models against their base counterparts using naturalistic eye-tracking data.

3. Key Contribution
The main contribution is a rigorous eye-tracking evaluation showing that more extensive Cantonese-specific training generally yields a better predictive fit for human reading behavior. The study also provides a novel insight that model rankings depend heavily on the specific information-theoretic measure being evaluated.

4. Methodology
The authors compare two within-family model pairs using naturalistic Cantonese eye-tracking data. They evaluate CKIP GPT-2 Tiny against its lightly adapted derivative JED351, and Qwen2.5-7B against the extensively adapted CantoneseLLM-7B. From these models, they extract four metrics. These are lexical surprisal \(S(w) = -\log_2 P(w)\), POS surprisal, entropy before the target, and entropy reduction.

5. Limitations
The study evaluates only two specific pairs of models, which may limit the generalizability of the findings across different LLM architectures. Furthermore, the metric-dependent rankings suggest that these information-theoretic measures might capture distinct cognitive processes. This makes it difficult to declare a single universally best model.

6. Critical Evaluation
A major strength of this work is its controlled within-family experimental design, which cleanly isolates the effect of Cantonese adaptation on gaze prediction. However, the mixed results across metrics, such as entropy reduction favoring the smaller CKIP model over the extensively adapted CantoneseLLM-7B, present a weakness. This indicates that larger or more adapted models do not uniformly capture the cognitive mechanisms underlying human reading.

LandmarkLens: Predicting and Presenting Effective Landmarks for Mixed-Reality Urban Exploration

Authors: Chu Li, Yotam Sechayk, Jared Hwang et al.
arXiv: 2608.30142v1

Summary

1. Research Question. How do navigation strategies and landmark attention differ based on a person's sense of direction, and can mixed reality and AI improve spatial learning for those who struggle with navigation? The paper aims to solve the problem of existing navigation tools prioritizing route efficiency over cognitive map building.

2. Positioning. Existing navigation tools optimize for getting to a destination quickly but fail to help users build long term spatial knowledge. This work bridges the gap between spatial cognition literature and modern AI by using eye tracking to understand individual differences in spatial awareness. It translates these cognitive insights into an applied mixed reality system powered by vision language models.

3. Key Contribution. The main contribution is LandmarkLens, a mixed reality navigation system that uses a vision language model to dynamically identify and highlight effective landmarks. The authors also provide empirical evidence that gaze behaviors and landmark preferences systematically differ between people with good and poor senses of direction. This is a novel approach showing that guiding visual attention via AI can help poor navigators acquire landmark level spatial knowledge.

4. Methodology. The researchers first conducted a VR eye tracking study with 20 participants to analyze gaze behavior and verbal landmark descriptions across four Tokyo neighborhoods. Based on the systematic differences found between good and poor navigators, they developed a mixed reality interface using a vision language model to predict and highlight navigation relevant landmarks. They then evaluated this system with eight poor navigators to measure improvements in scene recognition.

5. Limitations. The evaluation study only included eight participants with a poor sense of direction, which limits the statistical power of the findings. The research also relies on VR for the initial eye tracking study, which may not perfectly translate to real world outdoor navigation. Furthermore, the system focuses on landmark level spatial knowledge, which is only a first step toward broader spatial learning and route retention.

6. Critical Evaluation. The integration of eye tracking insights with vision language models is a strong and novel approach to accessible navigation. A potential weakness is that relying on VLMs for real time landmark detection in complex urban environments might face latency or robustness issues. Overall, it is a highly promising direction that effectively grounds AI assistance in cognitive science.

Predicting Radiologist Expertise from 3D Gaze Patterns During CT Interpretation

Authors: Leila Khaertdinova, Anna Anikina, Claudia Mello-Thoms et al.
arXiv: 2608.23836v1

Summary

1. Research Question. The paper aims to determine if the expertise level of a radiologist can be accurately predicted from their 3D gaze patterns during volumetric CT interpretation. The core research question is whether integrating visual search behavior into a deep learning model can provide an objective assessment of radiological skill.

2. Positioning. Prior eye-tracking research in medical imaging has predominantly focused on 2D image analysis. This work addresses the unexplored gap of applying gaze analysis to volumetric 3D CT scans for the purpose of expertise assessment. It bridges computer vision and human visual search behavior to evaluate medical reading skills.

3. Key Contribution. The main contribution is a novel gaze-informed transformer framework that classifies radiologist expertise using 3D CT reading sessions. It introduces two specific innovations for integrating gaze data into a DINOv2 vision transformer backbone. These are a learnable log-space bias applied to the self-attention mechanism and a gaze-weighted pooling technique for patch embeddings.

4. Methodology. The authors use a DINOv2 backbone to process volumetric CT data and integrate radiologist fixation patterns directly into the transformer architecture. Gaze data is incorporated through a learnable log-space bias applied to the self-attention layers and a gaze-weighted pooling of patch embeddings. The model was trained and evaluated on 182 CT reading sessions from five radiologists of varying experience levels.

5. Limitations. The evaluation dataset is relatively small, comprising reading sessions from only five radiologists. This limited sample size may restrict the generalizability of the model to broader clinical populations or different scanning protocols. Additionally, the abstract does not detail the computational overhead required for real-time or near-real-time gaze integration.

6. Critical Evaluation. This work presents a compelling intersection of eye-tracking and transformer architectures, achieving high predictive metrics with an ROC-AUC of \(0.91\) and an F1 score of \(0.86\). A major strength is the architectural innovation of embedding gaze data directly into the self-attention mechanism rather than treating it as a separate input stream. However, the reliance on a small cohort of five radiologists means the model needs validation across larger, more diverse groups before deployment in actual medical training or evaluation.

Matched Outcomes, Divergent Gaze: How Foveated MLLMs Search Compared to Humans

Authors: Mohamed Amine Kerkouri, Marouane Tliba, Aladine Chetouani et al.
arXiv: 2608.16514v1

Summary

1. Research Question
Do multimodal large language models (MLLMs) perform visual search like humans when given the exact same foveated visual input? The paper investigates whether models exhibit human-like serial search processes or rely on fundamentally different architectures.

2. Positioning
This work situates itself at the intersection of human cognitive modeling and multimodal AI evaluation. It challenges the assumption that matching spatial attention or task outcomes means an MLLM models human vision. The authors identify a gap in current evaluation metrics which fail to capture the temporal dynamics of the visual search process.

3. Key Contribution
The paper demonstrates a clear dissociation between task outcomes and gaze processes in MLLMs. While models achieve human or super-human accuracy and target acquisition efficiency, their scanpaths are low-entropy and highly self-consistent, unlike human serial search. This reveals that standard answer-alignment and saliency metrics are insufficient for certifying human-like vision.

4. Methodology
The researchers evaluate three general-purpose MLLMs on the COCO-Search18 dataset by driving them fixation by fixation through human-matched foveated views. They compare model behavior against human eye-tracking scanpaths across three axes. These axes are target presence decisions, target acquisition efficiency, and the temporal gaze process itself.

5. Limitations
The study focuses on three general-purpose MLLMs, which may not represent future architectures designed specifically for serial processing. The findings are constrained to the COCO-Search18 dataset and the specific foveation degradation regimes tested. Furthermore, the work highlights an inability to recover human-like search dynamics but does not propose a concrete solution to bridge this process gap.

6. Critical Evaluation
The strength of this work lies in its rigorous experimental design, specifically the fixation-by-fixation foveated input matching that isolates architectural differences from acuity limits. It provides a crucial critique of current evaluation metrics in gaze-alignment research. However, a weakness is that it primarily diagnoses the problem without offering architectural modifications to make MLLM search more temporally human-like.

EgoGazeLite: On-Device Egocentric Gaze Prediction for Token-Efficient Multimodal LLM Video Input

Authors: Matteo Stoiber, Niels Buus Lassen
arXiv: 2608.15614v1

Summary

1. Research Question
How can we achieve token-efficient egocentric video understanding in multimodal LLMs without relying on dedicated eye-tracking hardware? The paper aims to create a software-only gaze predictor that is accurate enough to maintain MLLM description quality yet light enough to run on-device.

2. Positioning
Prior work like GazeLLM reduces visual tokens by cropping video around the camera wearer's gaze, but this depends on physical eye-tracking hardware unavailable on consumer smart glasses. This work fills the gap by providing a pure software substitute that operates within the compute and power limits of a standard smartphone.

3. Key Contribution
The main contribution is EgoGazeLite, a lightweight dual-process gaze predictor for egocentric video. It eliminates the need for eye-tracking hardware by achieving performance statistically equivalent to ground-truth gaze crops while running in real time on consumer accelerators.

4. Methodology
The paper proposes a dual-process gaze prediction model designed for on-device execution. It processes egocentric video to predict gaze points, which are then used to crop the video before feeding it to an MLLM. This reduces the visual token count by roughly tenfold while using only \(15.7M\) parameters and \(6.71\) GFLOPs, achieving \(21.6\) ms per frame processing speeds.

5. Limitations
The abstract does not explicitly detail specific failure cases or environmental limitations, such as performance in poor lighting or highly dynamic motion. Additionally, equivalence is established primarily through automated metrics and LLM judges, which may not fully capture nuanced human evaluations of video descriptions.

6. Critical Evaluation
This work is a highly practical bridge between wearable technology and MLLMs, successfully removing hardware barriers for efficient video processing. Its primary strength is proving statistical equivalence to ground-truth gaze, though its reliance on automated LLM judges leaves room for validation against human perception.
